## What it does

Applies David Epstein's Range: the case for breadth over early specialization. Generalists win on novel, complex, open-ended problems because they bring analogies and frameworks from distant domains; specialists only win where the problem is stable and the rules never change (like a chess opening or a narrow golf swing). Covers the learning paradox (struggling on hard material transfers further), interleaving practice, broad sampling before commitment, and the 'match quality' search for the right kind of work.
