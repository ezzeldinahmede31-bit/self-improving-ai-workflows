# Bly Copywriter's Handbook

Distilled from Bob Bly's *The Copywriter's Handbook*: the working manual for
B2B/direct copy — briefs, USPs, feature-benefit translation, and the
mechanical craft (Johnson boxes, lifts, P.S. lines) that lifts response.

## Purpose

Ship complete, professional copy packages on deadline: brief → promise →
proof → offer, with every working part named and checkable.

## The craft (project order)

1. **Brief before words.** Audience, offer, objective, mandatory inclusions,
   tone limits, deadline — in writing, signed. Writing without a brief is
   revision roulette.
2. **Find the USP.** The one thing competitors cannot or do not say
   (strongest: unique benefit; then unique mechanism; then superior proof).
   No USP found = research continues, not writing begins.
3. **Translate features to benefits.** Every feature gets "which means..."
   until a buyer outcome appears; stack benefits by buyer priority (their
   ranking, not yours). Specifications support; benefits persuade.
4. **Assemble the persuasion architecture.** Headline (promise) → lead
   (expand + qualify reader) → body (proof stacking: demo, data, testimony,
   guarantee) → offer (explicit, fenced) → close (action + urgency + risk
   reversal). Working parts for response: Johnson box, lift letter/note,
   P.S. restating offer, order form as sales letter in miniature.
5. **B2B disciplines.** Multiple readers (user, approver, finance) addressed
   in one package; logic AND emotion (B2B buyers justify emotionally-chosen
   vendors rationally — feed both); long copy welcomed when complexity
   demands it; white papers/case studies as proof assets, not brochures.

## Verification

Package review: brief attached, USP in one sentence, every feature mapped
to a benefit, architecture complete (no missing close), B2B reader coverage
checked. Copy that "feels done" without the architecture is a draft.
