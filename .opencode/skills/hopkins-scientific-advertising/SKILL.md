# Hopkins Scientific Advertising

Distilled from Claude Hopkins' *Scientific Advertising* (1923, still
correct): advertising is salesmanship multiplied — every claim testable,
every shilling tracked. Opinions are abundant; tested knowledge is rare.

## Purpose

Replace creative arguments with experiments: small tests, measured sales,
then scale what proved itself — the origin of direct-response discipline.

## The laws (unchanged in a century)

1. **Advertising is salesmanship in print (pixels).** Write as the best
   salesman talks: specific claims, reasons-why, service to the buyer.
   Generalities ("best quality") are the mark of the amateur; the professional
   deals in particulars (what, how much, why, what happens next).
2. **Test small, then roll out.** Try headlines, offers, and copy on small
   samples before national spend (today: A/B budgets, holdouts, geo tests).
   Never scale an untested idea — enthusiasm is not evidence.
3. **Headlines decide.** Five times the readership: put the selling promise
   (or the news) in the headline, name the audience when it selects buyers,
   never cleverness that obscures the offer.
4. **Sampling and service sell.** Free trials, samples, demonstrations, and
   genuinely useful content (recipes, guides) outperform claims because they
   let the product prove itself. Give value first; the sale follows the proof.
5. **Track to the sale.** Every ad carries its measurement (codes then,
   attribution now): cost per result per variant, compared honestly. Kill
   losers fast regardless of seniority of opinion behind them.
6. **Psychology over art.** Layout serves reading (headlines, pictures that
   demonstrate, coupons/CTAs visible); artistry that distracts from the
   offer is expensive decoration. Respect the reader's time and intelligence.

## Verification

Campaign review: test plan with sample sizes stated, headline variants with
selection rationale, tracked measurement per variant, kill/scale criteria
pre-committed. Untested national-scale spend is rejected as gambling.
