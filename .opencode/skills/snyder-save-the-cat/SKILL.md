# Snyder Save the Cat!

Distilled from Blake Snyder's *Save the Cat!*: audiences reward same-but-
different — primal genres, a 15-beat skeleton, and a hero worth rooting for.
Structure first (it is rewriteable); pages second.

## Purpose

Outline any script/video fast on the beat sheet, then write pages that land
because the skeleton already works.

## The Blake Snyder Beat Sheet (feature shape; compress proportionally)

1. **Opening Image + Theme Stated** (1, ~5): the world before; someone
   voices the theme the hero will learn.
2. **Set-Up** (1-10): hero's flaw, world, stakes; **Save the Cat** moment —
   hero does something likable early (rooting interest is engineered, not
   hoped for).
3. **Catalyst** (~12) + **Debate** (12-25): life-changing knock; hero resists
   change (relatable hesitation).
4. **Break into Two** (~25): decision made, new world entered.
5. **B Story** (~30): the relationship/theme carrier begins.
6. **Fun and Games** (30-55): the PROMISE of the premise delivered (trailer
   moments live here); **Midpoint** (~55): false victory or false defeat
   raising stakes.
7. **Bad Guys Close In** (55-75): pressure mounts, internal + external.
8. **All Is Lost + Dark Night** (~75): defeat + whiff of death; theme
   echoed — the lesson crystallizes.
9. **Break into Three** (~75-85): synthesis — hero fuses old flaw-fix with
   new world to act.
10. **Finale** (85-110): execute the plan, prove the theme changed them,
    transformed world shown in **Final Image** (mirror of Opening).

## Logline + genres (sell it, shelve it right)

- Logline: irony + compelling mental picture + audience + cost — one
  sentence that makes the movie unmissable. Test: strangers repeat it back.
- Primal genres (Monster in the House, Golden Fleece, Out of the Bottle...):
  name yours — genre sets the audience's expectation contract; breaking it
  unknowingly reads as broken.

## Verification

Outline review: all 15 beats present with page targets, save-the-cat moment
named, midpoint polarity stated, finale proves theme per the logline. Pages
before beats is drafting backwards — outline first, always.
