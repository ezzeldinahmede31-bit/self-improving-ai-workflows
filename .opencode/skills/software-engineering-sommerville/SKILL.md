# Software Engineering (Ian Sommerville)

Sommerville's Software Engineering is the comprehensive survey of the discipline. This skill applies its process and practice knowledge to real projects.

## Processes and models

- Software processes (waterfall, incremental, agile) shape how work flows; choose by the project's uncertainty.
- Plan-driven suits stable, safety-critical work; agile suits evolving requirements.
- A process is a means, not a goal; adapt it to the team and the context.

## Requirements engineering

- Requirements engineering elicits, analyzes, specifies, and validates what the system must do.
- Functional and non-functional requirements are both contracts; state them precisely.
- Change is inevitable; a requirements process manages change instead of denying it.

## Design and modeling

- Architecture decisions structure the whole system; capture them explicitly.
- System modeling (use cases, class, sequence, state) communicates the design.
- Design for evolution: the code must survive the requirements that change.

## Testing and evolution

- Testing shows the presence of defects, never their absence; plan the levels (unit, integration, system, acceptance).
- Software evolves after delivery; maintainability is a design goal, not an accident.
- Professional and safety standards apply; the engineer is responsible for the consequences.
