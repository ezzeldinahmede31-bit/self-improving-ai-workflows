# Zinsser On Writing Well

Distilled from William Zinsser's *On Writing Well*: good writing is clear
thinking made visible — strip clutter, find your humanity, and serve the
reader with craftsmanship and warmth.

## Purpose

Produce nonfiction (articles, docs, memos, about pages) that people finish:
clear, brief, human, and exact.

## The principles (applied in revision order)

1. **Strip clutter ruthlessly.** Delete every word doing no work
   (very/really/quite/actually, throat-clearing openers, doubled phrases).
   Shorter is better until meaning thins — then stop. Most drafts lose 30%
   with zero information loss; that 30% was fog.
2. **One idea per sentence, one job per paragraph.** Unity: sentences serve
   the paragraph's point; paragraphs serve the piece's point. Beginnings
   orient, endings resonate — never waste either on filler.
3. **Active voice, strong verbs.** People doing things (not "utilization was
   effected"): verbs carry sentences, adjectives and adverbs ride along.
   Jargon translated on first use; technical terms earned, never flaunted.
4. **Humanity allowed.** Warmth, humor, and the writer's presence are
   features (even in technical writing) — readers trust people, not
   institutions. Write to ONE person, not "users".
5. **Audience-shaped structure.** Lead with what matters to THEM (not
   chronology of your work); endings that land (quote, image, return to the
   opening).

## Verification

Revision checklist per piece: clutter pass (word-count delta recorded),
one-idea scan, verb audit (passive count down), humanity check (would a
person say this aloud?), ending resonance test. Unrevised second drafts are
first drafts with confidence issues.
