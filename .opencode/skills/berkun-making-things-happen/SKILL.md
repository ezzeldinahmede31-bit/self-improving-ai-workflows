# Berkun Making Things Happen

Distilled from Scott Berkun's *Making Things Happen*: projects ship because
someone drives decisions, communication, and schedules — leadership as a
daily practice, not a title.

## Purpose

Keep a project moving: decisions made, everyone informed, schedule honest —
the three jobs that never delegate themselves.

## The three jobs (daily)

1. **Drive decisions.** Every open question gets an owner + a date. Decide
   with incomplete data (waiting is also a decision — with worse odds).
   Record decision + rationale + dissent in one place; revisit only on new
   evidence, never on relitigation. Killed ideas get funerals (written
   rationale) so they stay dead.
2. **Communicate relentlessly.** Status that answers: done / doing / blocked
   (with owner per blocker). Meetings with purpose, agenda, and notes — or
   cancelled. Bad news travels fast UP (surprises are the real failure);
   context flows DOWN continuously (goals, priorities, why). No side-channel
   decisions: if it is not written where the team looks, it did not happen.
3. **Own the schedule honestly.** Milestones as ranges with confidence;
   critical path identified and protected (delays there delay everything —
   staff it first). Track velocity against plan weekly; replan openly when
   reality diverges (hiding slip compounds it). Buffer belongs to the
   project, not to padding individual estimates.

## Power tools (use weekly)

- **Triage ruthlessly:** must/should/could per milestone; cut could-items at
  the first slip, not the last.
- **Prototypes over debates:** build the contentious piece small and early;
  working software settles arguments documents cannot.
- **Post-mortems without blame:** what happened, why (process causes),
  what changes (owner + date). Same failure twice = process failure, owned
  by leadership.

## Verification

Friday check: decision log current (no ownerless questions older than a
week), status truthful (blockers named with owners), schedule reflects
measured velocity. Any "green" that required squinting is red next week.
