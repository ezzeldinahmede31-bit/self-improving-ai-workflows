# McKee Story Design

Distilled from Robert McKee's *Story*: plot is the writer's choice of
events arranged to dramatize the controlling idea — structure is not formula,
it is the audience's expectation of meaning earning its payoff.

## Purpose

Build narratives (scripts, ads, keynotes, brand films) where every beat
turns value and the climax proves the idea — no filler, no unearned emotion.

## The design (outside-in: idea → acts → beats)

1. **Controlling idea (one sentence).** Value + cause: "Justice triumphs
   BECAUSE the hero sacrifices pride." Every scene argues for or against
   it; scenes arguing neither are cut. Write it first, test the ending
   against it last.
2. **Inciting incident.** The event that unbalances life and demands the
   core quest — within the opening, no throat-clearing. Weak inciting =
   audience never leans in; strengthen until inaction costs more than action.
3. **Progressive complications.** Each beat turns a value (love/hate, trust/
   betrayal, hope/despair) FARTHER than before — rising, never lateral.
   Gap dividing expectation from result creates the emotion; reversals beat
   continuations for attention.
4. **Crisis, climax, resolution.** Crisis: the impossible choice revealing
   true character (the most telling decision under maximum pressure).
   Climax: action (not dialogue) that irreversibly resolves it and PROVES
   the controlling idea. Resolution: brief — meaning lands, then out.
5. **Beats, scenes, sequences, acts.** Beat = smallest value turn; scene =
   unity of action aiming to turn once; sequence = chain toward a greater
   turn; act = movement ending in a point of no return. Design top-down,
   write bottom-up: beats must turn on the page, not just in the outline.

## Verification

Story review: controlling idea stated, beat map with a value turn per beat,
inciting strength judged, crisis/climax identified with the proof named,
filler scenes cut with reasons. Emotion without structure is manipulation
that audiences smell.
