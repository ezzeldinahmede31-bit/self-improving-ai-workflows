# Engineering Org Design

Transfers the organization design chapters of Camille Fournier's The Manager's Path to shaping engineering teams that deliver.

## When to use
- A team grows past the size where one manager can know everyone's work.
- Delivery is blocked by unclear ownership, ambiguous reporting lines, or handoffs.
- Planning a reorg, a new team, or a promotion into management.
- Cross-team work keeps colliding because no team owns the interfaces that connect the work.

## Team shape for the work
- Size teams around the work's coupling: a tightly coupled product area stays in one team; loosely coupled areas can split.
- Keep a team small enough that the manager knows the work and can review it honestly.
- Choose structure by mission (product, platform, or service ownership), not by floor plan or headcount.
- Give every team a clear owner for its part of the system so nothing is owned by everyone and no one.

## Reporting lines and accountability
- One manager per engineer, with a defined span that stays small enough for real 1:1s.
- Accountability follows ownership: the team that owns the service owns its uptime, quality, and roadmap.
- Keep the chain short; every extra layer slows decisions and dilutes feedback.
- Make the manager accountable for the team's output and the engineer accountable for their area.

## Growth, reorgs, and promotion into management
- Grow by splitting a team when it is healthy, not when it is broken; a broken team split into two just duplicates the dysfunction.
- Promote into management people who want the job and can learn it; technical seniority alone is not a management skill.
- During a reorg, state the new structure, the reasons, and the new owners explicitly; ambiguity breeds politics.
- Keep the promoted manager on a ramp: define the new expectations before the title changes.

## Cross-team interfaces
- Define cross-team interfaces: who owns what API, which team consumes it, and how changes are communicated.
- Assign one team as owner and one as consumer for every shared system; change requires the owner's consent.
- Resolve cross-team disputes at the lowest level that can decide, with a clear escalation path.

## Verification discipline
- Write the target org as a diagram with named owners and interfaces; check every system has exactly one owner.
- Confirm each manager's span and each engineer's reporting line on the diagram.
- After a reorg, verify delivery is unblocked and no ownership gaps remain.
