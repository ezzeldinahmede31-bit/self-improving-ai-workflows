# Seeking SRE (David N. Blank-Edelman, ed.)

Seeking SRE collects the hard-won experience of practitioners who built reliability programs. This skill applies their lessons to team culture and practice.

## The culture of reliability

- Reliability is a property of the whole system, including the team and its processes.
- SRE earns authority through data and service; the relationship with development is partnership, not policing.
- Hiring and growing SREs means valuing judgment, debugging, and calm under fire.

## Incidents and on-call

- On-call is a burden to be designed: the rotation, the escalation, and the relief all matter.
- Incident management is a discipline with roles and a timeline; practice it before the fire.
- Blameless postmortems move the conversation from people to systems.

## Monitoring and automation

- Monitoring exists to answer questions quickly, not to collect data.
- Automation removes toil so the team can work on the hard problems.
- The alert is a contract: a human reads it and knows what to do.

## The organizational questions

- An SRE team needs the mandate to say no to unreliability and the authority to enforce budgets.
- The error budget is the shared currency that lets development ship and SRE sleep.
- Scale the team with the service; the practices must survive growth.
