# High Output Management

Grove's classic is the productivity system behind modern tech leadership: an
individual's output is the sum of the team's, and a manager multiplies it through
leverage — choosing activities whose output amplifies many people's work, and
running meetings, reviews, and training to make them pay off.

## When to use

- When you (or a team) are busy but not productive — activity without leverage.
- Designing meetings, reviews, OKRs, or training that must produce real output.
- Any time the question is "how should I spend my time and my team's time."

## The framework

### 1. Managerial leverage
- Your output = (output of your team) + (output of the teams you influence).
- Leverage = output / time spent. A high-leverage activity shapes many people's
  work for hours with a small investment of yours (a decision, a standard, a
  well-run meeting, a training session).
- Audit your week: classify every block by leverage (high/medium/low) and shift
  time toward the high-leverage blocks. Say no to the rest.

### 2. Output-oriented meetings and decisions
- Every meeting has a purpose: a decision to make or information to exchange.
  End with the decision and the owner + date, not a discussion.
- For decision meetings: state the issue, the options, the trade-off, then decide.
- One-on-ones: the report sets the agenda; the manager listens and prepares.

### 3. Task-relevant maturity (situational leadership)
- Match your management style to how mature the person is for the *specific task*:
  low maturity → direct, structured guidance; growing maturity → coach and
  discuss; high maturity → delegate with clear objectives and checkpoints.
- The same person may be mature for one task and not another — judge per task, not
  per person.

### 4. Performance review and goals
- Set objectives with key results that are measurable (MBO/OKR style): few, public,
  and reviewable. Ambiguous goals produce ambiguous effort.
- Reviews are an honest, documented assessment of output against the agreed
  results, delivered in person with a development plan — not a surprise or a
  negotiation.

### 5. Training and motivation
- Training is the highest-leverage investment in a team: teach how to do the work
  better, once, and it multiplies forever.
- Motivation is environmental: remove obstacles, give clear feedback, and tie work
  to outcomes the person cares about (see `engineering-management-path`).

## Verification
- Your week's time audit shows a majority of time in high-leverage blocks.
- Every recurring meeting ends with a recorded decision, owner, and date.
- Goals are written with measurable key results and reviewed on a schedule.
