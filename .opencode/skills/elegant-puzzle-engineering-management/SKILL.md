# An Elegant Puzzle — Managing Engineering as a System

Larson's thesis: management is a field where you engineer the human and
organizational system with the same rigor you apply to code. Every problem is a
puzzle with a lever; find the lever, pull it, measure.

## When to use

- Designing or reshaping a team, an org, or a set of teams.
- A team that is overloaded, misaligned, or not improving — diagnose then act.
- Planning a roadmap, running 1:1s, giving feedback, or doing reviews.

## The systems view
- A team is a system with inputs, outputs, and feedback. Before fixing symptoms,
  name the system boundary and the lever that moves it (see `thinking-systems`).
- Prefer **stable systems** over **dynamic ones**: small, predictable changes beat
  constant reactive motion. When you must change direction, change it once,
  clearly, and measure.
- Change has a cost to absorb — do not pile a second change on top of a team that
  is still absorbing the first.

## Team design (the org puzzle)
- Shape the team for the work's dependencies: small teams (2-3 engineers) move
  fast; larger teams trade velocity for coordination. Choose deliberately.
- Match scope to capacity: a team needs a mission it can actually finish, or it
  demoralizes.
- Growth paths must exist inside the shape — a flat team stalls senior growth.

## The management craft
- **1:1s** are the primary information channel: ask what is hard, what is blocked,
  what the person wants — and take action on the answers. Never cancel them.
- **Feedback** is a tool, delivered promptly and specifically, aimed at behavior
  and impact, not identity (see `engineering-management-path` for the mechanics).
- **Roadmaps** are decisions, not schedules: state the goal, the constraints, and
  what you will deliberately not do this quarter.

## Projects and load
- Protect the critical path: identify the one binding constraint on delivery and
  relieve it (see `thinking-theory-of-constraints`).
- Keep the team's queue short and visible; an overloaded team produces quality
  debt that compounds.
- When scope is too large, split the problem into puzzles — one per team — and
  sequence them by dependency.

## Mentoring and growth
- Grow people through slightly-too-hard work with a safety net, not through
  lectures.
- Praise publicly and specifically; correct privately with a fix in mind.

## Verification
- A team shape has a stated rationale and a measurable outcome (throughput,
  on-time delivery, retention).
- Every 1:1 produces an action item you follow up on.
- After a change, confirm the system is stable before the next change.
