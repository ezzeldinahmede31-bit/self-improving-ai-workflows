# Strunk Elements of Style

Distilled from Strunk & White's *The Elements of Style*: the micro-rules
that separate clean prose from muddy — small, absolute, and checkable by
inspection.

## Purpose

Apply the final polish pass: every sentence shorter, stronger, and exactly
as long as its meaning requires.

## The elementary rules (mechanical checks)

1. **Omit needless words.** Vigorous writing is concise: delete throat-
   clearing ("in order to" → "to", "due to the fact that" → "because",
   "at this point in time" → "now"). If removing a word changes nothing,
   it stays removed.
2. **Active voice default.** "The committee approved" beats "Approval was
   given by the committee." Passive only when the actor is unknown or the
   receiver matters more — a conscious choice, never a habit.
3. **Keep related words together.** Subject-verb-object adjacent; modifiers
   next to what they modify (dangling participles rewritten, not excused).
   Readers shouldn't hold half a sentence in memory waiting for its verb.
4. **Definite, specific, concrete.** Prefer the exact noun and strong verb
   over the vague and the nominalized ("decided" not "came to a decision").
   Numbers and names beat adjectives.
5. **Which vs that (and the comma rules).** Restrictive (that, no commas)
   vs non-restrictive (which, commas): the distinction changes meaning —
   honor it. Commas for parentheticals, series clarity, and introductory
   elements; semicolons joining independent clauses.

## Style principles (judgment checks)

- **Place emphatic words at the end** (the period position has weight).
- **Parallel construction** for parallel ideas (mismatched forms signal
  muddled thought).
- **Consistent tense and tone** within a piece; shifts flagged and justified.

## Verification

Polish pass outputs the diff stats (words cut, passives fixed, nominalizations
converted) plus the three hardest sentences before/after. Unmeasured
"tightening" is vibes.
