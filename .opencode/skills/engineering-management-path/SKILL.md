# The Manager's Path — Engineering Leadership

Fournier's book walks the whole leadership ladder — tech lead, first-line manager,
managing managers — and names the job as *people and process*, done on purpose:
support, feedback, and creating conditions where engineers do their best work.

## When to use

- Moving into (or already in) a tech-lead or management role.
- Fixing broken one-on-ones, feedback, or career conversations.
- Building or reorganizing a team, or managing a manager.

## The path, role by role
- **Tech lead**: you are still an engineer but you own delivery — break the work,
  unblock, and protect the team's focus. Your code quality matters less than the
  team's throughput.
- **Manager**: your output is the output of your reports. Remove obstacles, set
  context, hold the bar, and make the team's work legible upward.
- **Managing managers**: your reports manage people now; coach the coaching, watch
  the health of the whole org, not individual tickets.

## The operating practices

### 1. One-on-ones are the core
- Regular, dedicated time for the report's agenda (not a status update).
- Ask what is going well, what is stuck, what they want next. Listen most; advise
  least.

### 2. Feedback done right
- Timely, specific, behavior-based: what happened, what the impact was, what to
  change. Praise in public, correct in private.
- Separate the person from the behavior; never make feedback a personality verdict.

### 3. Career conversations
- Understand what the person values (growth, stability, mastery, title) before
  proposing a path.
- Give an honest gap assessment and a concrete plan to close it; adjust over time.

### 4. Hiring and onboarding
- Hire for the job's real needs; a strong candidate who does not fit the team's
  missing skills is a wrong hire.
- Onboarding is a project: clear goals, a buddy, and a first-week win that builds
  momentum.

### 5. Managing upward and the org
- Learn what your manager needs from you and deliver it predictably.
- For org change: diagnose the real structure, communicate the "why" repeatedly,
  and manage the human cost of change — people move at their own pace.

## Verification
- Every report has a regular one-on-one and a written career direction.
- Feedback was delivered and received in a way that changed behavior (ask the report).
- A team outcome improved measurably because of a management action you took.
