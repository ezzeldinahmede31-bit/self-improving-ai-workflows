# Managerial Leverage and OKRs

Transfers Andrew Grove's High Output Management to managers who must turn their time into the team's output.

## When to use
- A manager's calendar fills with low-value activity while the team's output stalls.
- Goals are stated without measurable results, so progress is opinion.
- The user asks how to spend management time or how to run an OKR loop.

## Managerial leverage
- Output of a manager equals the output of the people and decisions they influence; their own hands-on output is secondary.
- Leverage is the multiplier: an hour spent where it changes many people's hours is worth more than an hour doing the work yourself.
- Compute leverage before scheduling: ask what the multiplier of each activity is, then allocate time to the highest.
- Uniqueness rule: spend time only where you add value no one else can; delegate the rest.
- Time is the manager's scarcest resource; protect large blocks for high-leverage work.

## OKR loop
- Objectives are qualitative and memorable; key results are measurable and time-bound.
- Keep key results few and honest; three to five per objective is enough, and each must be checkable.
- Cascading: team key results roll up to the manager's objective, but let the team write their own key results so they own them.
- Review on a cadence: compare actual results to the key results, explain gaps, and update for the next period.
- A key result that cannot be measured is not a key result; rewrite it until it is a number.

## Applying leverage to goal setting
- Use the OKR loop as the mechanism that converts the manager's leverage into the team's measurable output.
- Allocate review time proportional to the leverage of the goal, not the loudness of the complaint.
- When leverage is low on a goal, either delegate it or drop it; do not keep spending high-value time on low-multiplier work.

## Verification discipline
- List the week's activities with their leverage multipliers and confirm high-leverage items got the most time.
- Check every objective has a measurable key result with an owner and a review date.
- After each review period, record actuals versus key results and write the gap explanation.
