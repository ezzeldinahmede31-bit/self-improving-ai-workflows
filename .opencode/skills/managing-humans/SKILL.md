# managing-humans

Michael Lopp (Rands) writes about the human side of engineering leadership: management is mostly communication, honesty, and attention to individuals. Use this skill to run the rituals and conversations that make a team trust its lead and do their best work.

## Core principles
- The one-on-one belongs to the report; it is their space for what they need, not a status meeting.
- Feedback is a craft: be specific, be direct, and deliver it with care and timing.
- Hire for evidence of behavior, not charm; interviews must produce comparable facts about candidates.
- Trust is earned through consistency, honesty, and being present when things are hard.
- The team's health is the manager's product; morale and psychological safety are measurable outputs.
- Managing upward is part of the job: translate team reality into language leadership can act on.

## Key patterns
- Regular, protected one-on-ones with a standing agenda and no last-minute cancellations.
- The feedback sandwich avoided; state the observation, the impact, and the ask plainly.
- Structured hiring loops with role-specific questions and a shared scoring rubric.
- Delegation with intent: assign outcomes, agree on success criteria, and give the person room to own it.
- Skip-level meetings and anonymous surveys to hear what the direct reports will not say in person.
- A written, visible career and growth conversation each cycle, not only during raises.

## Applying this to n8n/automation/code
- Use one-on-ones to learn how people really use the automation library and where it burdens them.
- Run the delivery postmortem as a blameless conversation so automation failures improve trust, not erode it.
- Treat workflow reviews as feedback conversations: specific, kind, and aimed at the author's growth.
- Delegate an automation domain (say, integrations or reporting) fully, with clear success criteria.
- Keep a decision log so management questions have documented answers instead of recollections.

## Hard rules
- Never cancel or reschedule a one-on-one without a direct apology and a make-up slot.
- Never give public corrective feedback; deliver it privately and promptly.
- Never hire on impressions alone; require evidence gathered through a structured process.
- Never promise what the team cannot deliver; say the hard truth early.
