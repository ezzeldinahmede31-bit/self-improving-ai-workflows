# Dicks Storyworthy

Distilled from Matthew Dicks' *Storyworthy*: great storytellers are made by
a daily practice (Homework for Life) plus architecture (five-second moments,
stakes, transformation) — not by waiting for dramatic lives.

## Purpose

Produce an endless supply of tellable true stories and shape them so
audiences laugh, cry, and remember the point.

## The practice (daily + per-story)

1. **Homework for Life (daily).** Each evening, record the day's most
   storyworthy moment in one line (spreadsheet/note). Criteria: it changed
   you slightly, surprised you, or made you feel strongly. Five minutes a
   day compounds into hundreds of tellable stories a year — storytellers
   are collectors first, performers second.
2. **Find the five-second moment.** Every story has one heartbeat moment
   where everything changes (the diagnosis, the yes, the fall). Build
   BACKWARD from it: everything before raises stakes toward it, everything
   after shows its cost. No five-second moment = anecdote, not story.
3. **Stakes must be personal.** Small stakes, deeply felt (losing a child's
   trust for an afternoon) beat big stakes, abstractly held. State what the
   teller stood to lose IN THEIR terms — audiences lend emotion against
   personal collateral only.
4. **Transformation, stated.** The teller ends different from the start
   (belief, behavior, understanding) — say the change out loud at the end
   (the "moral" earned, never preached). Untransformed teller = unmoved
   audience.
5. **Craft the telling.** Start in the action (in medias res, then backfill
   minimally); scenes over summaries (dialogue, beats, sensory detail);
   humor as surprise+truth (never at the vulnerable's expense); endings that
   land past the laugh/cry (one reflective beat after the peak).

## Verification

Story review: homework log exists (recency checked), five-second moment
named with timestamp in the telling, stakes personal and stated,
transformation explicit, opening-in-action confirmed. Stories without a
moment are drafts, not stories.
