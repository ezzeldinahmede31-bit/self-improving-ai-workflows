# Software Engineering: A Practitioner's Approach (Roger S. Pressman)

Pressman's text is the practitioner's comprehensive reference on the software lifecycle. This skill applies its process, design, and quality guidance to real projects.

## The process

- Process models (waterfall, incremental, spiral, agile) define the rhythm; match the model to the risk.
- The process is a framework; the team fills it with discipline.
- Every project should state its process and why it fits.

## Requirements and design

- Requirements are the foundation; analyze them before designing.
- Design concepts (abstraction, modularity, information hiding, coupling, cohesion) guide the structure.
- The design is the blueprint; it must be testable and maintainable.

## Quality and testing

- Quality is designed in; reviews and testing find what slipped through.
- Test levels and strategies map to the risk each part carries.
- Measure the process and the product; the metrics make improvement possible.

## Project management

- Planning, estimating, scheduling, and tracking keep the project on course.
- Risk management names the threats before they become fires.
- Communication and measurement are the glue of a well-run project.
