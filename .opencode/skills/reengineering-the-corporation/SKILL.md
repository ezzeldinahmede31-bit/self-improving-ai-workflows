# Reengineering the Corporation (Hammer & Champy)

Transfers the reengineering discipline for when incremental improvement is not enough: challenge every assumption, reorganize around outcomes, and rebuild the process from a blank sheet.

## When to use
- A process that is slow, costly, or broken despite optimization.
- Redesigning a workflow from zero rather than patching the old one.
- Breaking assumptions that everyone treats as immutable.

## The principles
1. Question the assumptions behind the current process; most steps exist for reasons that no longer hold.
2. Organize around outcomes, not tasks: one role owns the whole result instead of a fragment.
3. Have the people who use the output perform the work (self-service, direct handling) instead of passing it through hand-offs.
4. Capture information once, at the source, and share it; do not re-key or re-collect it.
5. Put the decision point where the work is done, with built-in control.
6. Treat geographically dispersed work as if it were collocated; treat parallel work as concurrent, then connect the pieces.

## Discipline
- Redesign the process before re-tooling it; automating a broken process makes it faster and worse.
- Design the to-be process around outcomes and measure it against the baseline.
- Bring the people who run the process into the redesign; they hold the assumptions being challenged.

## Verification discipline
- Compare the redesign against the measured baseline on time, cost, and quality.
- Test the new process end-to-end on a real case before scaling it.
- Confirm the eliminated steps truly served no remaining purpose; keep the ones that do.
