# Staff Engineer — Leadership Beyond the Management Track

Larson's book: Staff+ engineers are paid for *leverage* — the product of a decision's
scope and the number of people it affects — not for hours worked. The title comes
responsibility to steer, teach, and keep the whole system moving in the right
direction.

## When to use

- Operating as a technical leader across teams (staff/principal/tech-lead scope).
- Preparing for or navigating a Staff+ role.
- Making technical decisions that affect more than your own ticket.

## The leverage mindset
- Rank the options by **scope × impact**: the highest-leverage work is the decision,
  design, or infrastructure change that multiplies many engineers' output.
- Learn to say no to high-effort, low-leverage work — the visible but unimportant
  task is the trap.
- Leverage compounds: a decision, standard, or tool that outlives you keeps paying.

## The four paths (pick deliberately)
- **Architect**: own technical direction, keep the architecture coherent.
- **Tech Lead**: own delivery and the team's execution.
- **Right hand**: be the executive's trusted technical partner on hard org-wide
  problems.
- **Solver**: go where the hardest, most ambiguous problem is and fix it.
You are not stuck with one — but know which you are playing now.

## The operating practices

### 1. Build and use context
- Map the organization: who decides what, what are the real priorities, what has
  been tried and failed before. Context beats cleverness — a great idea with no
  context goes nowhere.
- Read the room: proposals land based on the decision-makers' concerns, not your
  enthusiasm. Frame in their terms.

### 2. Steer decisions without authority
- Influence through well-formed proposals (problem, options with trade-offs,
  recommendation, cost), pre-socialized before the meeting.
- Document decisions and the "why" so future staff engineers inherit your context
  (see `tradeoff-and-postmortem-documenter`).
- Say "no" or "later" gracefully with the trade-off made visible.

### 3. Keep quality high while moving fast
- Protect the critical few quality bars (security, correctness, deployability) and
  let the rest move.
- Use the org's own gates as leverage (see `build-gates-pipeline`): a gate is an
  agreement everyone already signed.

### 4. Mentor and multiply
- Review designs, not just code; teach the reasoning, not only the answer.
- Leave behind standards and tools so the team does not depend on you being present
  to stay good.

## Verification
- For any stretch of work, state the leverage (scope × impact) you are choosing and
  the path you are on.
- A decision you steered is adopted and documented with its rationale.
- After a quarter, ask: did the team get faster and safer because of this role?
