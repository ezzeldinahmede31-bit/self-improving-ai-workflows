Range makes the counter-case to 10,000-hours overspecialization: on complex, changing, open-ended problems, generalists beat specialists. This skill applies that evidence to how we solve problems, learn, and design.

## When breadth wins
- Novel problems with no stable rules: specialists apply one pattern and stall; generalists port analogies from unrelated fields.
- Complex systems where pieces interact: you need the map of the whole system, not deep expertise in one node.
- Fast-changing environments (tech, marketing, automation): yesterday's playbook is today's liability.

## The learning paradox
- Struggling on difficult, varied material transfers to new problems far better than smooth, repetitive drilling.
- Interleave practice: mix topics instead of blocking one topic to exhaustion. The painful mixing is the point — it builds discrimination.
- Deliberate testing beats re-reading. Attempt the problem before you have been shown the answer.

## Match quality
- Before committing deeply to one specialty, sample broadly. Try many kinds of work early so you can find the one whose demands best fit your abilities.
- Match quality is a driver of long-run success; you cannot compute it without sampling.

## Applying to problem-solving and builds
- When stuck on a hard problem, list distant domains that solve a structurally similar shape (queuing, immune systems, kitchens, air traffic), then lift their mechanisms. Analogy across domains is a generalist superpower.
- When a workflow problem resists the standard n8n pattern, ask how a different industry would wire it, then adapt.
- Hold two or three mental models from different fields at once instead of one deep playbook.

## Hard rules
- Never claim a specialist answer is wrong purely because it came from one domain; evaluate the mechanism, not the source.
- Never substitute breadth for the depth a stable, well-defined task genuinely requires; where rules are fixed and feedback is fast, focused practice wins.
- Never pre-commit to a specialty without sampling first; match quality beats early start.
